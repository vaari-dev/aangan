A launcher for Android TV and Google TV

The home screen your TV should have come with.

Aangan replaces the stock home screen's ads and recommendations with your apps, the sky outside and the days your family celebrates. No sign-in, no tracking, no cost.

What it stands for

Four promises, kept in the code.

Small enough to read in an afternoon, so none of this needs trust.

O

Open

Every line is on GitHub under the MIT licence: plain Java, no libraries. Fork it, audit it, change it.

P

Private

No ads, analytics, crash reports, account or location permission. Family birthdays never leave the TV. The only outside service is the weather for your city.

M

Minimal

One small app, one job. On an ordinary day nothing moves, so it uses no processor time while you watch, and it checks the weather only while it's on screen.

C

Yours to arrange

Choose the dock's four apps, hide the rest, pick your city, add family days, move or hide festivals. With the remote, or from your phone.

Your apps

Your apps, and nothing else.

Four favourites in a dock, every other app in a grid. No sponsored rows, no "continue watching", no autoplaying trailers.

  • The dock stays as you set it, never reordered by what you watch.
  • Hold OK on any app to put it in the dock or hide it.
  • The focused app glows in its own colour.
  • Opens with the Home button, and when the TV turns on.

The sky outside

Weather and air you can act on.

Time, temperature and air quality in the corner. ▲ then OK for the whole day.

  • Pick a large city or type a few letters. Old names like Bombay or Gurgaon work.
  • The next hours, the high and low, sunrise, sunset, UV and the moon.
  • A 36-hour PM2.5 trend against your country's safe limit: CPCB in India, US EPA elsewhere.
  • The best time to air out the house.

The days that matter

Festivals and family days, drawn by hand.

A countdown to the next festival sits by the date. On the day, a scene plays behind your apps: diyas for Diwali, the moon through a chalni for Karwa Chauth, balloons and a greeting by name on a birthday.

  • Nineteen scenes, from Lohri's bonfire to Eid's lanterns to Christmas snow. See the calendar.
  • All of Diwali week and Navratri glow.
  • Scenes move only while the home screen is showing.

The forecast

Your city's coordinates, rounded to two decimals, go to api.open-meteo.com and air-quality-api.open-meteo.com, at most every 30 and 60 minutes, only while the home screen is showing.

City search

Letters typed in city search go to geocoding-api.open-meteo.com when you pause. Picking from the large-city list sends nothing.

Your phone

The setup page is served by the TV on your Wi-Fi, only while Settings is open, at an address with a fresh secret. It loads nothing from the internet.

Set up from your phone

Typing with a remote is slow. So don't.

Open Settings on the TV and scan the code. Your phone's browser opens a page served by the TV: choose the city, set the dock, edit birthdays and anniversaries, move or hide festivals. The TV updates as you go.

  • No app to install, no account, no cloud in between.
  • Nothing loads from the internet.
  • It stops when you leave Settings.
