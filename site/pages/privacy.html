Privacy policy

We receive nothing from the app. Here's exactly what it does.

Aangan has no servers, accounts, analytics or advertising, so its makers never receive information about you. This is what stays on your TV, and what the app sends to a weather service for you.

In short

  • We receive no personal data. No account, analytics, crash reporting, advertising or server.
  • Your settings stay on your TV, including family names and dates, excluded from Android's cloud backup. A backup file you export goes only where you save it.
  • One outside service: Open-Meteo, for your city's forecast and air quality, and for city search. Google Play counts the rounded coordinates as approximate location collected; see below.
  • No location permission. Aangan uses the city you choose.
  • The source is public, so you can check: github.com/vaari-dev/aangan.

Who we are

Aangan is a free, open-source (MIT) app published by Vaari (vaari-dev on GitHub). "We" means its makers and publishers. As we receive no data from the app, we are not a controller or processor of personal data it handles on your TV.

What stays on your TV

What leaves your TV

Three kinds of request, all over HTTPS to Open-Meteo, an open weather service. None contains an identifier for you or your TV. Like any internet request, they reveal your network's IP address to Open-Meteo; see its terms and privacy.

RequestWhat's sentWhen
Weather forecast
api.open-meteo.com		Your city's latitude and longitude rounded to two decimals (about 1 km) and its time zoneAt most every 30 minutes, only while the home screen is showing
Air quality
air-quality-api.open-meteo.com		The same rounded coordinates and time zoneAt most every 60 minutes, only while the home screen is showing
City search
geocoding-api.open-meteo.com		The letters you typed in the search boxWhile you search, when you pause typing. Picking from the large-city list sends nothing.

The requests identify the app as Aangan and carry no device ID, advertising ID, account, app list or family data. The code is in Weather.java and Place.java.

Apps you open from Aangan follow their own privacy policies.

Google Play's data safety

Google Play counts data an app sends to another service as "collected", even when its makers never receive it. In those terms:

Data typeCollectedSharedWhy
Approximate location: your chosen city's coordinates, rounded to about 1 kmYes, sent to Open-Meteo for each forecastNoApp functionality: the weather and air quality. Required, as the weather needs a city (Gurgaon until you choose one).
What you type in the city searchYes, sent to Open-Meteo while you searchNoApp functionality: finding the city. Optional.

Both go over HTTPS. We keep nothing, so there's nothing for us to delete; Open-Meteo's handling is in its terms. No other data type leaves the TV.

The Home-button service

Most TVs don't let you choose a home screen, so Aangan includes an optional accessibility service that makes the Home button open Aangan. Android asks you to confirm before it's turned on. All it does:

  • The Home button. It watches only the Home key, and opens Aangan. Every other button passes through unseen.
  • The TV's own home screen. It is told only when the built-in home screen comes to the front (as at start-up), and opens Aangan instead. It receives no events from other apps.
  • Nothing on screen. It can't read any window (canRetrieveWindowContent="false"), so it can't see what you watch, type or open.
  • Nothing kept or sent.

It follows this fixed rule only, and Aangan works without it. Switch it off any time in the TV's accessibility settings. Its configuration is in home_button.xml and its code in HomeButton.java.

The phone setup page

While Aangan's Settings is open, the TV serves a small web page on your home network for changing settings from your phone. It's the only time Aangan accepts a connection.

  • It starts when Settings opens and stops when you leave. The screen stays on, so the screensaver doesn't close it, until 20 minutes after Settings opens or your last change.
  • Its address holds a new random secret each time, shown only on the TV as a QR code. Requests without it get a "link expired" page.
  • The TV serves it directly over your Wi-Fi, not through the internet or any server of ours. It loads nothing from elsewhere, sets no cookies, and asks your browser not to cache it or send referrers.
  • Export downloads a backup file to your phone: city, family days, festival changes, dock and hidden apps. It goes only where you save it, never to us or a cloud. Import reads it back, for example on a new TV.
  • It uses plain HTTP, as a device on a home network can't get an HTTPS certificate without a cloud service. Someone watching traffic on the same network could see what you enter; on a network you trust, that's no different from the TV itself.

What Aangan never does

  • Show ads, or use any third-party library, advertising, analytics or otherwise.
  • Send anything to us, or sell or share your data. Only the Open-Meteo requests above leave the TV.
  • Use your TV's location, microphone or camera.
  • Track what you watch, which apps you open, or for how long.
  • Read other apps' screens or notifications.
  • Phone home: there is no Aangan server.

Your control

  • See or change your data in Settings, or from your phone.
  • Delete it: remove a family day or festival change in Settings, or everything with Settings → Apps → Aangan → Clear data or by uninstalling. Nothing remains elsewhere, apart from backup files you exported.
  • Stop all network use by disconnecting the TV. Aangan keeps working, minus fresh weather.

We hold no data about you, so we can't look up, export or delete anything for you; it's all on your TV. To keep a copy, use Export on the phone setup page.

Children

Aangan is suitable for all ages. Its makers receive no information from anyone, including children, and it has no ads, purchases or social-media links.

This website

This site has no scripts, cookies, analytics or embedded content, and loads nothing from other servers. GitHub Pages hosts it and, like any host, may log your IP address and browser details; see GitHub's privacy statement. GitHub also serves release downloads.
