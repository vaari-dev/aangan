Questions

Frequently asked.

Can't find your answer? Ask on GitHub.

What is Aangan?

A home screen, or launcher, for TVs running Android TV or Google TV. It replaces the stock home screen, with its rows of ads and recommendations, with your apps, the weather and air quality, and a countdown to festivals and family birthdays. It's free, open source and collects no data.

Does it remove the ads from my TV's home screen?

It replaces the screen they're on. With Aangan as your home screen, you see your apps and nothing promoted. Apps themselves, such as streaming services, still show whatever they show inside them.

Which TVs does it work on?

It should work on any TV or streaming box with Android TV or Google TV 9 (Pie) or later: Sony Bravia, TCL, Hisense, Philips, Sharp, Xiaomi Mi TV and Mi Box, Chromecast with Google TV, Nvidia Shield and others. It's developed and tested on Sony Bravia TVs. It doesn't run on Fire TV, Tizen (Samsung), webOS (LG) or Roku.

Do I need developer mode or a computer?

No. Install it with the free Downloader app, and use the welcome's Open with Home to make the Home button bring it up. See how.

Why does it need an accessibility service?

Most TVs offer no setting to choose a different home screen. The accessibility service is the only way, without a computer, for an app to answer the Home button. Aangan's service watches the Home key and the moment the TV's own home screen appears, and does nothing else: other buttons pass straight through, and it can't read anything on screen. It's optional; the privacy policy has the details.

Will it slow my TV down?

No. Aangan is about 150 KB, has no libraries, and on an ordinary day nothing on its screen moves, so it uses no processor time while it sits there or while you watch something else. The weather is fetched only while the home screen is showing, at most every 30 minutes.

My phone says the setup page is "not secure". Is it safe?

Yes, on your home Wi-Fi. Browsers warn about any page that doesn't use HTTPS. The setup page is served by the TV itself, and a device on a home network can't get an HTTPS certificate without routing through a cloud service, which Aangan avoids on purpose. Choose to continue; most browsers then remember that choice for the TV's address. The page and what you type never leave your network, and the page stops the moment you leave Settings on the TV.

The QR code doesn't open on my phone.

Make sure the phone is on the same Wi-Fi as the TV, not on mobile data or a guest network. Keep Aangan's Settings open on the TV while you use the page. Some routers isolate devices from each other; if so, add the dates on the TV instead.

Why doesn't it use my location?

It doesn't need to. You choose your city once, and Aangan has no permission to find out where the TV is. The forecast request sends your city's coordinates rounded to about a kilometre.

Where are my family's birthdays stored?

Only on the TV, in Aangan's private storage, which is excluded from cloud backup. There's no account and no copy anywhere else. Uninstalling Aangan deletes them.

Our family celebrates a festival on a different day.

Move it. In Settings or on the phone page, choose the festival and change its date, or hide it for the year. See how.

Can I add my own festival or a regional one?

Not in the app yet. Suggest it on GitHub, or add it to the calendar file yourself; it's one line. Meanwhile, you can add any yearly day as an anniversary.

How do I hide an app or change the top row?

Hold OK on the app. Hidden apps come back from the Settings tile's menu. More in the guide.

How do I go back to the TV's own home screen?

Uninstall Aangan from Settings → Apps, or switch off its Home-button service in the accessibility settings. Aangan doesn't change or disable anything on your TV, so the stock home screen is exactly as you left it.

Is it on Google Play?

It's being prepared for Google Play. Until then, install it with Downloader. Both are signed with the same key, so you'll be able to switch without losing anything.

Who makes it, and how is it free?

It's made by Vaari as an open-source project, originally for a family's own TVs. It costs nothing to run because there are no servers: your TV does all the work. There's nothing to sell, including your data.

Can I trust a download from GitHub?

Every release is built from the public source and signed with the same key, whose fingerprint is on the install page. Android refuses an update signed with any other key, so a tampered copy can't replace yours.

How can I help?

Report problems and suggest festivals on GitHub, try it on a TV we haven't, or send a pull request. The developer guide is the place to start.
